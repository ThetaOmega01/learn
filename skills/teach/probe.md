# Probe

You can't teach into the learner's zone of proximal development without knowing where its edges are, or aim the teaching without knowing what they are reaching for. Two unknowns, two tools.

## Level: map the edge with `quiz`

This is a **mapping** job, not a spot-check: locate the frontier where what the learner reliably knows turns into what they don't, along every strand the lesson will depend on. It takes as long as it takes.

- **Bracket every strand.** For each relevant strand you need a **floor** (something at that level they get right) and a **ceiling** (something they get wrong or don't know). The edge sits between them; one side alone tells you almost nothing.
- **All-correct means the questions were too easy.** A run of right answers is a floor with no ceiling. Escalate until something breaks.
- **Binary-search the edge.** After a right answer, jump difficulty up sharply. After a miss, narrow back in.
- **One miss is one coordinate, not a cue to teach.** Probe around it to tell a slip from a narrow gap from a systematic misconception. Misconceptions matter most: a confidently-held wrong model must be dislodged, not topped up, so dig into its extent.
- **Bound by relevance.** Map every strand the plan will lean on; skip corners it won't touch.
- Probe questions carry no `concept` tag: they map the learner, they don't schedule reviews.

Done when, for each goal-relevant strand, you can state concretely what the learner has and where it ends.

## Goal: make it concrete with `ask`

What the learner wants is often hard for them to articulate ("understand LLMs" can mean ten different things, and each changes what you teach). Interrogate the vision with `ask` until it is concrete. In course mode the chapter and `COURSE.md` goal frame it; in sprint mode the application is the goal.
